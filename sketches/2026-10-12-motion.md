# Motion: things arrive rather than appear

Milestone / step: after milestone 11's realignment (#447)
Date: 2026-10-12

## What I am about to do

Brian, 2026-10-12: "Would be nice to add some modern animations,
features, etc. to the style to make it feel a little more modern and
crisp and efficient."

Crisp means short and purposeful: motion that says where something came
from, never motion for its own sake, and nothing over a quarter of a
second. Six places:

1. **Moving between pages** cross-fades instead of flashing white:
   `@view-transition { navigation: auto; }`. The bar, the pane and the
   strip are named, so they hold still while the centre changes.
2. **A dialog** rises a few pixels and fades in, its backdrop fades.
3. **A row's menu** drops in from its button.
4. **Results** fade in as they are swapped into `#hits`.
5. **A job** arriving in the strip fades in.
6. **A button** presses down by a pixel.

## How I expect it to work

All CSS, in `gui/theme.py`, no script:

- Entry is `@starting-style`: the browser gives a newly rendered element
  (or one whose `display` changes, as a `<dialog>` opening or a
  `<details>`'s content showing) the starting style's values and then
  transitions to its own. So a dialog `showModal()`ed by `dialog.js`, a
  menu opened by its `<summary>`, a hit swapped in by htmx, and a job
  drawn by `progress.js` all animate with no event handled anywhere.
- Page changes are a cross-document view transition, which Chromium
  performs between two same-origin pages that both opt in. Every page
  carries the stylesheet, so every page opts in. Each named element is
  matched old to new; an unchanged pane looks unchanged.
- Exit animation is not attempted: `dialog.js` removes a closed dialog,
  and a removed element has nothing left to animate.
- **Reduced motion**: the existing rule shortens every animation and
  transition to 0.01ms, but `*` does not select the view transition's
  pseudo-elements, so `@view-transition { navigation: none; }` goes
  under the same media query.

## What I expect to be uncertain or difficult

- A view transition holds the old page's picture while the new one
  loads; on a slow route (a document page computing matches) the
  cross-fade could read as lag. Measure a document page.
- `@starting-style` on `.hit` also fires on the first page load, which
  is harmless, and on every htmx swap of `#hits`, which is the point.
- Firefox supports `@starting-style` but not cross-document view
  transitions; it navigates as now.
- Whether a named pane keeps its scroll and its open folders across a
  transition: it is a picture of the old page, then the new page's own
  pane, which the server draws in the same state from the cookie.

## What actually happened that I did not expect

- **The bare-class guard caught the first draft.** I wrote the motion
  as its own block, with a second `.hit` and `.menu-items` rule; that is
  exactly #425's shape, and `test_no_class_is_given_a_second_meaning`
  refused it. The transitions went into the existing rules; only the
  `@starting-style` blocks are new.
- Measured, not assumed: the dialog goes from opacity 0 and 8px low to
  1 and 0px over about 200ms; a hit arrives at 0.07 and is 1 by 400ms;
  a menu at 0 then 1; a navigation reports `pageReveal.viewTransition`
  and the pane is drawn throughout. With reduced motion there is no
  view transition and the dialog is opaque within 50ms.
- The worry about a slow document page reading as lag did not hold: it
  loads in 0.44s with matches computed, and the cross-fade is 0.15s.
- The walk script's first click on a collection hit its name, which is
  a link, and navigated rather than opening the folder - a reminder
  that the name is the scriptless way in (#430's unit).
- Injections: seven, all caught.
